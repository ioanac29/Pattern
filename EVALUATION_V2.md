# Pattern Evaluation — V2

## Goal

Evaluate whether the changes made after the V1 evaluation improved Pattern's evidence accuracy, scoring, coverage, and language.

The same 4-interview fleet operations dataset used for V1 was used again so the outputs can be compared directly.

## Critical Insights

### Insight 1

**Pattern output:** Operational users prioritize knowing whether vehicles are ready and want actionable guidance instead of more dashboards or charging details.

**Pattern scores:** Evidence Strength 4/5 · Strategic Importance 5/5 · Coverage 2/4

**V1 comparison:** Coverage improved from 4/4 to 2/4 and every source counted now has a visible supporting quote.

**My assessment:** This is an improvement, but the evidence still doesn't really fully support the finding as written. Interview 1 strongly supports it, but the quote from Interview 2 — "The plan is usually wrong by 9am." — is about operational unpredictability, not wanting actionable guidance instead of dashboards or charging details.

Also, the title is still combining several related ideas into one finding. Pattern has improved the traceability of the evidence, but it still seems able to count a quote that is related to the topic without actually supporting the core claim.

### Insight 2

**Pattern output:** Distrust in data caused by conflicting information across systems limits confidence and adoption.

**Pattern scores:** Evidence Strength 3/5 · Strategic Importance 5/5 · Coverage 1/4

**V1 comparison:** Coverage improved from 3/4 to 1/4. Pattern now only counts the Sustainability Director, whose quotes directly support the finding.

**My assessment:** This is much more accurate than V1. The coverage now reflects the actual evidence and the supporting quotes are traceable to the participant making the claim.

I still think Evidence Strength 3/5 is too high for a finding supported by one participant, even when that participant mentions the issue several times. Multiple quotes from one person shouldn't carry the same weight as evidence repeated across several interviews.

The title also goes slightly beyond the direct evidence by saying the issue "limits adoption." I see that more as a potential consequence and would put it under Why it Matters rather than in the finding itself.

### Insight 3

**Pattern output:** Operational users express skepticism toward AI concepts but show interest in specific predictive insights.

**Pattern scores:** Evidence Strength 3/5 · Strategic Importance 4/5 · Coverage 1/4

**My assessment:** I think Pattern identified a meaningful finding here and the two quotes directly support it. The coverage is also accurate.

The problem is the language. The evidence comes from one interviewee, but the title generalizes this to "Operational users." It should make the limited evidence clear rather than implying this is a broader user pattern.

Again, Evidence Strength 3/5 feels too high for evidence from only one participant.

## Contradictions

### Contradiction 1

**Pattern output:** One dispatcher expresses skepticism toward AI but also wants predictive alerts about vehicle problems.

**My assessment:** This is much better than V1. Pattern identified the same genuine tension, but it no longer generalizes one participant's comments to "users." The title correctly says "One dispatcher," and both sides are supported by direct quotes from that participant.

I think this is working as intended.

### Contradiction 2

**Pattern output:** Operational users ask for visibility while leadership interprets need as prediction and problem prevention.

**My assessment:** I still don't think this is a genuine contradiction.

The supporting evidence from operational users actually says "I don't need another dashboard" and "Showing me a problem isn't really helping me. I need to know what to do." Leadership says "The biggest opportunity isn't better reporting. It's preventing problems."

Those positions seem more aligned than opposed. Both are pointing towards moving beyond visibility or reporting towards something more useful or proactive.

Pattern has improved the wording by not claiming that operational users reject prediction, but it is still creating a contradiction from different perspectives on what may actually be the same underlying need.

With only four interviews, I also don't think there is enough evidence to establish an operational-users-vs-leadership divide.

## Weak Signals

### Weak Signal 1

**Pattern output:** One dispatcher relies heavily on intuition and often detects issues before automated systems.

**Pattern scores:** Confidence 2/5 · Coverage 1/4

**My assessment:** I think this is working well. The finding is directly supported by the quote, the title correctly limits the finding to one Dispatcher, and the low confidence and 1/4 coverage reflect the amount of evidence available.

V1 described this as "Operational users use intuition," so the evidence-proportional language has improved.

V1 also incorrectly presented a researcher observation as a participant quote in another weak signal. That problem did not appear in this V2 output.
