# Pattern Evaluation Dataset — 10 Fleet Electrification Interviews

## Interview 1 – Fleet Manager, Regional Distribution

Context:
Manages 42 trucks, 11 electric.
Electric vehicles have been in operation for approximately 18 months.
Responsible for daily vehicle availability and driver allocation.

Quote:
"My question every morning is basically the same: can I send this truck out or not?"

Uses several systems during morning planning.

Quote:
"I can see battery percentage, range, charging status, all of that. But then I'm still the one deciding whether I trust it enough to assign the truck."

Says information is generally available but requires interpretation.

Quote:
"More information isn't really the problem. I have plenty of information."

Would like clearer guidance when something needs attention.

Quote:
"If something is wrong, tell me what I need to look at. Don't make me investigate five screens."

Researcher note:
Participant repeatedly returned to decision-making rather than data availability.

Does not describe major problems with data accuracy.

Quote:
"I wouldn't say I don't trust the numbers. It's more that I don't always know what they mean for today's route."


## Interview 2 – Dispatcher, Urban Delivery

Context:
Plans daily routes for approximately 25 vehicles.
Works directly with drivers throughout the day.
High number of short routes and schedule changes.

Quote:
"The schedule we make in the morning is almost never the schedule we finish with."

Frequently adjusts routes manually.

Quote:
"A driver calls in sick, traffic changes, a customer isn't ready. The system can't know all of that."

Uses vehicle information when replanning but says operational knowledge matters more.

Quote:
"You learn which routes are risky and which drivers use more energy. That's just experience."

Asked whether predictive warnings would be useful.

Quote:
"Maybe. If it's right often enough. But if it starts warning me about everything, I'll stop looking."

Researcher observation:
Participant appeared more interested in reliability than in whether the feature was described as AI.

Quote:
"I don't care if you call it AI or magic. Just don't make me check something that isn't actually a problem."


## Interview 3 – Transport Operations Manager

Context:
Responsible for mixed diesel and electric fleet across two depots.
Has used electric vehicles for three years.
Oversees fleet managers and dispatchers.

Says the organization initially struggled with EV operations but processes have improved.

Quote:
"The first year everybody wanted another dashboard because nobody understood what was happening."

Now feels the bigger issue is coordination.

Quote:
"We've got the data now. What we don't always have is the same understanding of it."

Different teams sometimes interpret vehicle status differently.

Quote:
"Operations says the truck is fine, charging says it needs another hour, and then somebody has to decide who's right."

Researcher note:
Potential trust issue, but participant framed it primarily as interpretation and ownership rather than inaccurate data.

Asked about prediction.

Quote:
"Knowing something will go wrong in three hours is useful only if I can still do something about it."

Quote:
"Early is good. Too early can just become noise."


## Interview 4 – Fleet Manager, Long-Haul Transport

Context:
Manages 17 electric heavy-duty trucks.
Routes are planned several days ahead.
Charging is tightly integrated with depot operations.

Very positive about existing monitoring tools.

Quote:
"Honestly, I think the dashboard is good. I use it every day."

Does not want the system making operational recommendations.

Quote:
"I want the information. I don't necessarily want the system telling me how to run the fleet."

Feels local knowledge is difficult for software to capture.

Quote:
"We have routes where I know the driver can make it even when the estimate looks tight."

Would still value earlier warnings about technical failures.

Quote:
"If you know the battery is developing a problem, obviously I want to know before the truck stops."

Researcher note:
Participant distinguishes between technical prediction and operational decision-making.


## Interview 5 – Sustainability Manager

Context:
Responsible for electrification targets and sustainability reporting.
Does not manage daily fleet operations.
Works across several markets.

Finds it difficult to compare results between countries.

Quote:
"I can get three reports that are technically correct and still have three different answers."

Says definitions differ across systems and teams.

Quote:
"One market calculates utilization one way, another market does it differently. Then we spend the meeting discussing the number instead of the result."

Believes this damages confidence in reporting.

Quote:
"Once people start questioning where the number came from, the whole conversation changes."

Researcher observation:
Strong frustration around governance and definitions rather than vehicle-level data accuracy.

Would like more standardized metrics.

Quote:
"I don't need more KPIs. I need to know that the five we use mean the same thing everywhere."


## Interview 6 – Dispatcher, Construction Fleet

Context:
Works with 30 vehicles, including 8 electric trucks.
Routes and assignments change frequently due to construction-site delays.

Rarely uses the main fleet dashboard.

Quote:
"I probably open it twice a week."

Gets most information through drivers and depot staff.

Quote:
"If I need to know whether a truck is ready, I call someone."

Asked why.

Quote:
"It's faster. And they know what happened during the shift."

Has received automated notifications but finds many irrelevant.

Quote:
"Half the alerts are things I can't do anything about."

However, one recent battery warning was useful.

Quote:
"That one saved us. We moved the truck before it became tomorrow morning's problem."

Researcher note:
Participant laughed when asked whether they would like more notifications.


## Interview 7 – Head of Fleet Digitalization

Context:
Responsible for digital fleet tools across approximately 600 vehicles.
Works with product, IT, and operations teams.
Not involved in daily dispatch.

Believes current tools are too reactive.

Quote:
"We are very good at telling customers what already happened."

Wants to move toward prediction.

Quote:
"The value is knowing which five vehicles need attention tomorrow, not showing 500 vehicles today."

Believes customers may not explicitly request predictive capabilities.

Quote:
"If you ask people what screen they want, they'll describe a better version of the screen they already have."

Researcher note:
Strong belief in proactive products. No direct evidence presented that operational users share this view.

Acknowledges prediction must be reliable.

Quote:
"One bad prediction is understandable. Twenty unnecessary warnings and you've lost them."


## Interview 8 – Fleet Manager, Municipal Services

Context:
Manages refuse collection vehicles.
Fleet includes 14 electric vehicles.
Routes are highly repetitive.

Says range prediction has become reliable enough for daily operations.

Quote:
"Two years ago I checked range constantly. Now I barely think about it."

Primary concern is unexpected vehicle downtime.

Quote:
"The expensive part isn't knowing there's a fault. It's finding out when the truck is already supposed to be outside."

Would like earlier warning of likely technical problems.

Quote:
"If you could tell me on Tuesday that this truck probably shouldn't be planned for Thursday, that's useful."

Does not want detailed explanations.

Quote:
"I don't need to understand the model. I need enough confidence to change the plan."

Researcher observation:
Participant appears comfortable acting on prediction without understanding how it is generated.


## Interview 9 – Driver

Context:
Drives an electric distribution truck.
Has driven electric vehicles for approximately two years.
Does not use fleet management dashboards.

Receives some warnings directly in the vehicle.

Quote:
"Most of the time I know something feels different before a warning comes up."

Describes noticing changes in consumption and charging behavior.

Quote:
"You drive the same truck every day. You notice when it's suddenly using more battery on the same route."

Sometimes reports issues that later disappear.

Quote:
"I've told them something was wrong and then the workshop found nothing."

Would welcome earlier technical warnings.

Quote:
"If the truck knows something is starting to fail, of course tell me."

Researcher note:
Potential overlap between human intuition and predictive signals. Accuracy of participant's own predictions is unknown.


## Interview 10 – Director of Transport Operations

Context:
Responsible for operational performance across several depots.
Oversees both conventional and electric fleets.
Makes investment decisions for digital fleet tools.

Concerned that the organization is investing in technology faster than teams adopt it.

Quote:
"We keep buying tools and then discovering six months later that people are still using spreadsheets and phone calls."

Does not think lack of training is the main problem.

Quote:
"You can train someone ten times. If the tool doesn't help them make a decision, they'll go back to what works."

Interested in predictive capabilities but cautious about business value.

Quote:
"Prediction sounds great in a presentation. I want to know what decision changes because of it."

Quote:
"If I know a vehicle has a seventy percent chance of failing next week, what exactly do I do differently today?"

Researcher observation:
Participant is not rejecting prediction. Questions whether predictive information is actionable enough to justify investment.
