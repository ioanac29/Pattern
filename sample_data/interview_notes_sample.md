Interview 1 – Fleet Manager (Electric Trucks)

Context:
Managing ~80 electric vehicles across 3 depots.

Main challenges:

- Keeps talking about "vehicle readiness" rather than charging.
- Doesn't seem particularly interested in charging technology itself.
- Concerned about whether trucks are available when drivers arrive.

Quote:
"I don't wake up thinking about chargers. I wake up thinking about whether the truck is ready."

Mentioned that they currently check multiple systems in the morning.

Workflow today:
- Check fleet platform
- Check charging status
- Sometimes call depot manager
- Sometimes call driver

Feels like a lot of manual verification.

Interesting observation:
When discussing future improvements, immediately shifted conversation away from dashboards.

Quote:
"I don't need another dashboard."

Repeated several times that visibility isn't enough.

Quote:
"Showing me a problem isn't really helping me. I need to know what to do."

Seems frustrated by alerts that don't lead to action.

Mentions:
- Too many notifications
- Most notifications not urgent
- Sometimes misses important ones

Potential tension:
Wants fewer notifications but also wants to be warned earlier.

Quote:
"If everything is urgent, nothing is urgent."

Not sure whether root issue is alert volume or alert quality.

Interview 2 – Dispatcher

Context:
Responsible for daily operations and vehicle allocation.

Observation:
Conversation constantly returns to operational disruptions.

Examples mentioned:
- Drivers arriving late
- Vehicles returning late
- Chargers occupied longer than expected
- Unexpected maintenance issues

Quote:
"The plan is usually wrong by 9am."

Interesting:
Very skeptical of optimization tools.

Quote:
"Everything looks optimized until real life happens."

Seems to value flexibility over efficiency.

Asked about AI.

Initial reaction:
"Honestly, AI feels like a buzzword."

Later in interview:

Quote:
"If it could tell me which vehicles are likely to become a problem later today, I'd use that."

Possible contradiction:
Rejects AI conceptually but likes predictive outcomes.

Currently relies heavily on experience and intuition.

Quote:
"I can usually tell when something is going wrong before the system does."

Interesting area to investigate:
Trust in human judgement versus automated recommendations.

Interview 3 – Sustainability Director

Context:
Responsible for electrification targets.

Main concern:
Operational teams don't always trust the data.

Several examples where different systems showed different status information.

Quote:
"The fastest way to lose trust is to show different answers to the same question."

Strong emotional reaction when discussing data quality.

Feels organization is too focused on adding capabilities.

Quote:
"We keep adding features but we're not fixing confidence."

Mentioned adoption concerns.

Interesting:
Doesn't believe adoption problems are training-related.

Believes trust is the bigger issue.

Quote:
"If people trust the information they'll figure out how to use the system."

Potential hypothesis:
Data confidence may be a bigger adoption driver than usability.

Not validated.

Interview 4 – Head of Digital Transformation

Context:
Leading digitalization initiatives.

Conversation focused on future opportunities.

Strong belief that predictive capabilities will become important.

Quote:
"Customers don't ask for prediction because they don't know what's possible."

Interesting:
Different perspective than operational users.

Feels customers often describe symptoms rather than underlying needs.

Example:
Customers ask for more visibility.

Interpretation from interviewee:
Maybe they actually want fewer surprises.

Quote:
"They say they want information. What they really want is certainty."

Potential contradiction:
Users asking for visibility while leadership interprets need as prediction.

Discussed AI extensively.

Not pushing generative AI.

More interested in:
- risk prediction
- anomaly detection
- proactive recommendations

Quote:
"The biggest opportunity isn't better reporting. It's preventing problems."

Observation:
Leadership seems more optimistic about advanced capabilities than operational users.
